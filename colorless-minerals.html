Facts About Colorless Minerals

Colorless minerals are minerals that look clear or white to the eye, even though they can have different internal structures and chemical compositions. They are still part of Earth’s building blocks and are found in rocks, soils, and even in your home!

Quartz

Clear or white crystals, often found in sand and rocks. They are very hard, measuring a 7 on the Mohs scale.

Calcite

Can be colorless; when scratched with a penny, it leaves a white streak. It is often found in limestone.

Gypsum

Often white or colorless, this mineral is soft and commonly used in plaster and drywall.

Talc

The softest mineral (hardness 1), it is a white mineral used in baby powder and cosmetics.

Salt (Halite)

Identified by its white or clear cubic crystals, you might find this in the ocean or in your kitchen.

Whether they are shiny (luster), break into cubes (cleavage), or have different crystal shapes, colorless minerals are everywhere. So next time you see a clear rock or crystal, you might be looking at one of Earth’s hidden treasures!

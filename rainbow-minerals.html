🌈 Nature’s Rainbow Minerals

Discover the sparkly, colorful world beneath your feet!

What are minerals?

Minerals are solid, naturally occurring substances found in the Earth. They are inorganic (not made by living things) and have a special, organized internal structure called a crystal structure. There are over 4,000 types of minerals!

Why are they multicolored?

Color comes from how atoms absorb and reflect light. Some minerals, like Labradorite, have tiny crystals that scatter light—this is a cool effect called labradorescence. Others get their colors from elements like iron, copper, or manganese.

Meet the Colorful Minerals

Fluorite

The glowing wonder! It can be purple, green, blue, or yellow. It even glows under a black light!

Labradorite

A type of feldspar that shows rainbow-like flashes when the light hits it just right.

Copper

A reddish-brown metal that can turn a cool green over time when exposed to air.

Pyrite

Famous as "Fool's Gold." It's golden-yellow and has a shiny, metallic luster.

Calcite

Can be white, yellow, or even red! It often leaves a different "streak" color on tiles.

How to Identify Minerals

Property What it means
Luster How it reflects light (glassy, metallic, or pearly).
Hardness How tough it is (1 for soft talc, 10 for hard diamond).
Cleavage How a mineral breaks (some split into flat sheets).
Streak The color of the powder it leaves behind when rubbed.

Why Minerals Matter

Minerals aren't just pretty—they help build our bodies (like calcium for bones) and make the things we use every day. From the graphite in your pencil to the lithium in your cell phone, minerals are everywhere!

Check your riverbeds, mountains, and even your backyard—you might find a piece of Earth’s history!

Next time you see a rainbow rock, remember: you're looking at Earth's natural artistry!
